Hamza walked into the library early on Monday morning, carrying a heavy bag of books and a small notebook full of questions about network security. He had spent the whole weekend reading about old ciphers, and he wanted to find out whether a clever combination of two simple methods could really keep a secret message safe. His teacher had explained that every cipher must be tested by someone who tries to break it, because a code that nobody has attacked is only a code that nobody has understood. So Hamza decided to build his own cipher, write a program to encrypt a message, and then play the part of the enemy who tries to read it without knowing the key. After several hours of careful counting and testing, he discovered that the longer the message became, the easier it was to find the hidden pattern, and he wrote this lesson at the top of his report in large letters.
